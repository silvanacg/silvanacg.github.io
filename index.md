    <p class="statement">
      I work at the intersection of atmospheric science, wildfire, and geomorphic hazards.
    </p>

    <p>
      I study post-fire debris flows and hydroclimate extremes by integrating radar reflectivity,
      rain gauge observations, reanalysis products, and climate projections into statistical
      and machine-learning frameworks.
    </p>

    <p>
      My goal is to translate complex climate signals into spatially explicit hazard insights
      that support resilience planning and evidence-based decision-making.
    </p>

    <section class="section" id="about">
      <h2>About</h2>
      <div class="card">
        <p>
          I’m a Ph.D. candidate in Geography and Climate Science. My research connects storm-scale
          precipitation structure with post-fire hazard outcomes, combining observational datasets
          with predictive modeling to map when and where risk is most likely to emerge.
        </p>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section class="section" id="methods">
      <h2>How I work</h2>

      <div class="card-grid">
        <div class="card">
          <h3>Observation-driven</h3>
          <p>
            I integrate radar reflectivity with rain gauge observations to characterize storm intensity
            and spatiotemporal structure relevant to post-fire debris-flow triggering.
          </p>
        </div>

        <div class="card">
          <h3>Machine learning + statistics</h3>
          <p>
            I use statistical and machine-learning frameworks to connect hydroclimate extremes and
            precipitation signals to hazard likelihood and spatial patterns.
          </p>
        </div>

        <div class="card">
          <h3>Climate-informed risk</h3>
          <p>
            I incorporate reanalysis products and climate projections to translate changing climate
            conditions into actionable, spatially explicit hazard insights.
          </p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </section>
